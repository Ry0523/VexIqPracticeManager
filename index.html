Generate finals

Finals teams are paired by their current qualification ranks (Level Up T12-T14): ranks 1 and 2 form seed 1, ranks 3 and 4 form seed 2, and so on. Alliances play in seed order, lowest seed first. The highest finals score wins.

After you create finals, qualification scores are locked. Finals scores appear at the top of the Teamwork leaderboard and do not change qualification averages.

The Event Partner chooses the advancing count. For events qualifying directly to Worlds, T13 requires at least five finals alliances (10 teams) per division when ten or more teams attend.

Robot versions

Mark a new robot when the team builds a substantially different design. Each score belongs to the robot in use when it was first posted (practice runs use the entered run time). Leaderboards count only each team's current robot; Cumulative leaderboard team details can compare versions. Saved scores are never changed, so you can add, back-date, edit, or remove versions at any time. Removing a version lets you either count its results for the previous robot or abandon them (kept for comparison, excluded from leaderboards).

ALL SAVED SESSIONS / COACHING

Cumulative leaderboard

Unpromoted student-entered practice / quick runs do not affect leaderboard scores or leaders used for weighting. They remain included in team details, trends, and robot comparisons for analysis. Coaches can promote saved runs from the Practice tab. Older runs without a saved role remain eligible.

Your team can earn up to 100 points: up to 25 for Skills, 25 for Teamwork, 40 for Notebook, and 10 for Interview. Skills and Teamwork points use a curve: points still rise with scores, but one unusually high score has less effect on other teams. The top team gets 25 points; a score of 0 gets 0. Notebook and Interview use the latest coach scores. Student reviews do not count. Missing scores add 0. The Events/Practice filter changes Skills and Teamwork only. Rankings can change when team scores change.

How scores are calculated

Skills and Teamwork use separate top scores. For either category: points = 25 × ln(1 + your score) ÷ ln(1 + the top score). If every team scores 0, everyone gets 0. For example, with a top score of 150, a score of 50 earns about 19.6 of 25 points instead of 8.3.

Teamwork uses each team’s best status-adjusted score. Disqualifications and no-shows count as 0. Skills uses each team’s best Driver plus best Autonomous score.

Notebook points use the latest coach score for each criterion, out of 64. Interview points use the latest coach score for each criterion, out of 12. Student reviews do not count.

Only scores from the robot marked Current count for Skills and Teamwork. These are shared team scores, not individual robot scores. This is a coaching comparison, not official event standings or an award decision.

COACHING / REVIEW HISTORY

Engineering notebook rubric

Review tool. Only coach reviews update the current score and Cumulative leaderboard, using the latest coach score for each criterion. Team members can review any team and manage their own student reviews. They can read feedback for their team and reviews they wrote.

Preparing your notebook

Your notebook tells the story of how your team designed, tested, and improved its solution throughout the season.

  • Document everything you do with your robot. Take pictures as you go.
  • Date and organize entries so your team and others can find them later.
  • Show who contributed and give credit when you build on someone else's ideas.
  • Record sketches, code, test steps, measurements, decisions, and changes as you go.
  • Explain what happened, what you learned, and how that learning shaped your next step.

COACHING / REVIEW HISTORY

Team interview rubric

Review tool. Only coach reviews update the current score and Cumulative leaderboard, using the latest coach score for each criterion. Team members can review any team and manage their own student reviews. They can read feedback for their team and reviews they wrote.

Preparing for the interview

A team interview is a short, friendly conversation, not a test. Judges want to hear your team's story in your own words.

  • Take turns speaking, listen to one another, and encourage every teammate to contribute.
  • You may look things up in your Engineering Notebook. No one person needs to know everything.
  • Use specific examples, details, and evidence. A story about something that failed and what you learned is useful too.
  • Practice asking and answering questions with teammates, and explain your robot to people outside your team.

Based on the VEX Team Interview Rubric v2.0. Guidance shows a description for each rating. Ratings: 2 Heard, with Specifics; 1 Heard; 0 Not Yet Heard. Use zero only after giving students an opportunity to address the criterion. Six criteria, totaling 12 points.
